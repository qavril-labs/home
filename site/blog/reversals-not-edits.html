Why we fix mistakes with reversals, not edits

By the QAVRIL LABS team · Published 2026-11-02

In many systems a mistake is fixed by editing the old entry. That makes the history look as though the mistake never happened, which is exactly what an auditor will ask about.

A reversal undoes the entry at its original amounts, and the correction is posted as a new entry. Both stay visible. It takes a few more clicks, and it means the books can always explain themselves.
