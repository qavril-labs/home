# Close the credibility gap before launch

The ERP Platform marketing site is honest about being early, but it reads as more finished than it is, and it gives a buyer no firm answer on price, go-live effort, data migration, security, or support. Three problems block a credible launch: the lead form is inert until the committed API and contact placeholders are replaced, present-tense product copy and machine-readable schema contradict the site's own "not available yet" list, and a visible "undefined" card sits on the requirements page. Beyond those fixes, the site needs stand-alone pages for pricing basis, security and data, migration and implementation, support and company identity, and the first market, plus an honest comparison with Zoho and Odoo. The conversion path also needs a consent record, operator notification, and a structured slot before any lead can be trusted. The notes below cover the static site in `E:/SaaS/site`, the lead API and storage code, and a benchmark against Zoho and Odoo. None of it was verified in a running browser, and the competitor prices are mostly unverified.

## Fix the launch blockers before adding anything

The most damaging issue is that the form does not work outside a developer's machine. The committed site config points at **`http://127.0.0.1:3001`** and a placeholder contact at `hello@your-domain.example` ([config.js](E:/SaaS/site/config.js)). In a deployed browser, that address is the visitor's own machine, so every submission fails. When `apiBase` is empty, the form falls back to a `mailto:` draft that is never stored, and the subject is always "Early access request", even for demo and trial requests ([main.js](E:/SaaS/site/main.js)). The fix is to set the real API and mailbox, fail the deployment loudly if either is still a placeholder, and remove or gate the silent fallback.

The placeholder domain `https://your-domain.example` also appears in every canonical, `og:url`, sitemap `<loc>`, robots line, and llms.txt link ([build-site-pages.mjs](E:/SaaS/scripts/build-site-pages.mjs), [robots.txt](E:/SaaS/site/robots.txt), [sitemap.xml](E:/SaaS/site/sitemap.xml)). Until the real domain is set, no canonical or sitemap URL is valid for search or AI retrieval. The **Organization** schema holds only a name and URL, and it has no logo, contact point, or address ([index.html](E:/SaaS/site/index.html)).

The product copy contradicts the site's own status page. [pending.html](E:/SaaS/site/pending.html) says the **ERP sign-in screen is not ready**, yet the homepage marks "Secure customer accounts" as built under Level 0. Pending also lists notifications, document storage, approvals, and data import as not ready, while product pages describe approvals as a live feature. The product pages use present tense ("Fast checkout with split payments", "Payslips and pay runs") and their only status signal is a single "Availability" line. The **SoftwareApplication** description says the product covers "finance, sales, inventory, purchasing and payroll" in the present tense ([index.html](E:/SaaS/site/index.html)), and llms.txt repeats that framing. A buyer who trusts the product page will assume a live module that the pending page says is not available. Every product feature and requirement card needs a text status tag ("Live", "In build", "Planned") that matches pending, and the schema and llms.txt must use the same wording.

Two smaller defects are visible to buyers. The last card on [requirements.html](E:/SaaS/site/requirements.html) renders the literal text **"undefined"** because its data entry has `today` and `proof` fields where `covers` is expected ([site-content.mjs](E:/SaaS/scripts/site-content.mjs)). The bot breadcrumbs also lack a name for the middle item because `SECTION_NAMES` has no `bots` key ([pip.html](E:/SaaS/site/bots/pip.html)).

## Give buyers the pages they need to decide

The site answers "what is it" and "why is it different" well, and it answers almost nothing a small-business buyer asks next. The pricing block says "Early-access pricing, agreed on a call" with a "Talk pricing" button, and the pending page adds that "there are no published price lists yet" ([index.html](E:/SaaS/site/index.html)). Competitors publish more. Odoo's pricing page shows per-user pricing with a monthly and yearly toggle, a free "One App Free" tier, and a statement that paid plans include hosting, support and maintenance, with the exclusions listed ([odoo.com/pricing](https://www.odoo.com/pricing), live fetch). Zoho One offers a "Calculate your savings" TCO link and a separate pricing FAQ ([zoho.com/one/pricing](https://www.zoho.com/one/pricing/), live fetch). The platform cannot publish numbers yet, but it can publish **the pricing model**: what drives price (company size, modules, country), what is included, what is excluded, what early-access terms are, and when money is taken. No contract terms exist at all today, so term length, cancellation, and data export on exit need a page.

Security and data need specifics. The site offers generic claims such as "Sign-in is protected" and "Each customer's data is kept separate" ([products/security.html](E:/SaaS/site/products/security.html)). The requirements page promises "region-pinned hosting, encryption at rest and in transit" and "clear uptime targets", but gives no region, no RPO or RTO, no uptime figure, and no retention period ([requirements.html](E:/SaaS/site/requirements.html)). A search of all HTML finds no mention of SOC 2, ISO 27001, GDPR, or a data processing agreement. The privacy notice is a draft that a lawyer must review ([privacy.html](E:/SaaS/site/privacy.html)), and there is no terms, acceptable-use, cookie, or accessibility page. Security also carries a commitment that is not yet earned: "Included with every plan, not sold separately", on a site with no plans ([products/security.html](E:/SaaS/site/products/security.html)).

Migration and implementation are the next two gaps. The FAQ says import tools are "on the roadmap" and never names a source system, file format, or opening-balance process ([faq.html](E:/SaaS/site/faq.html)). Service lines such as "Opening balances migration" and "Stock count on go-live" appear with no price, duration, or delivery steps. A short migration page and an implementation page with phases, a timeline range, and who does the work would answer the two questions that matter most to a buyer comparing a new system with QuickBooks or Tally.

Support, company identity, and the first market are missing. The only support statement is "We reply by email within one business day" on the booking form, with no hours, channels, or escalation ([book.html](E:/SaaS/site/book.html)). There is no About page, no founder or team, no legal entity, no address, and no phone number. The FAQ says the company is "starting with one or two markets" without naming them, while the examples use KES and a Nairobi shop ([faq.html](E:/SaaS/site/faq.html)). A buyer cannot tell whether their country is supported, and an assistant asked "does it support my country" finds no answer. Naming the first market, with its currency, payment rails, and tax rules marked live or planned, is the single change that unlocks the most queries.

Finally, the site has no honest comparison. No page names Zoho Books, Zoho One, Odoo, QuickBooks, Xero, Tally, or ERPNext, so a buyer comparing options gets no help deciding who should choose a different product. Competitor figures are mostly unverified: the Odoo US-dollar price comes from third-party listings, and the Zoho Books 14-day trial and 50K revenue threshold come from a search summary ([Capterra Odoo pricing](https://capterra.com/p/135618/Odoo/pricing/), [Zoho Books trial sign-up](https://www.zoho.com/books/kb/subscription/trial-signup.html)). A comparison page should only go out after the vendor pages are checked on a fixed date. Until then, the comparison should describe who the product is not for.

## Make the lead path complete, not just present

The site offers a booking modal, a home-page form, and a book page, and all three pass through the same lead API. Each has a gap that loses or muddles leads. Request types differ by form: the home page offers `early_access`, `demo`, and `partner`, the book page offers `demo`, `trial`, and `early_access`, and the modal offers only `demo` and `trial` ([main.js](E:/SaaS/site/main.js), [index.html](E:/SaaS/site/index.html), [book.html](E:/SaaS/site/book.html)). A trial request can therefore be made from the modal or book page but not from the home form, and the industry lists differ across the three forms. The modal also shows a "Preferred slot" select even when "Free trial" is chosen. The home form has no time-preference field at all.

The data layer cannot yet support follow-up. The API stores **no consent record**, though it checks `consent: true`, so the site cannot prove consent later ([app.ts](E:/SaaS/apps/api/src/app.ts), [0002_leads.sql](E:/SaaS/packages/db/migrations/0002_leads.sql)). No notification reaches the operator on insert, and the visitor gets no acknowledgement. The slot is stored only as text inside `message`, with no structured column and no timezone, and the `leads` table has no status column for "contacted" or "booked". There is no uniqueness rule on email, so a retry after a timeout creates a duplicate. Follow-up today is a manual export through `npm run leads:export`.

The rate limiter needs two changes. It is keyed on `request.ip` with `trustProxy` off by default, so behind a CDN every visitor shares one IP and the limit of five per ten minutes applies to the whole site. It also counts every attempt before validation, so a visitor who corrects a mistake or a bot that hits the honeypot uses up the allowance ([app.ts](E:/SaaS/apps/api/src/app.ts), [rate-limit.ts](E:/SaaS/apps/api/src/rate-limit.ts)). Error handling is generic too: every status other than 201 or 429 shows "Something in the form didn't land", although the API returns field-level details the client ignores.

The trial path needs honest wording. Trial access is a waitlist, and the success message says "reply by email to confirm your trial/slot" even for trial requests ([main.js](E:/SaaS/site/main.js)). The success state should read "You are on the trial waitlist. We email you when it opens," and it should give a reference. The modal also has an accessibility gap: its errors appear only in the browser's native bubble and a generic line, with no `aria-invalid` and no per-field text.

## Win search and AI answers with names, schema, and linked pages

The search layer is sound in structure but thin in language. Every detail page carries a unique title, but titles use bare module or trade names such as "Restaurants and cafés | ERP Platform", with no category term such as "accounting software" or "POS system" and no "ERP" in the industry H1 ([restaurants.html](E:/SaaS/site/industries/restaurants.html)). The homepage title is the strongest on the site. Open Graph and Twitter tags appear **only on the homepage**, so a shared product, industry, or bot link shows no image on 52 of 53 pages. Structured data stops at BreadcrumbList on the generated pages, with no Product, Service, or HowTo markup, and the blog's JSON-LD lists only **4 of 8 posts**, with fragment URLs and no dates or author ([blog.html](E:/SaaS/site/blog.html)). Each blog article should get its own URL, `datePublished`, `dateModified`, and an author.

Several high-intent terms have no page. Nothing targets "ERP for small business", "What is an ERP", "ERP with POS", pricing basis, or a market such as Kenya, and multi-currency sits under Projects as a build-log page rather than a product page ([projects/multi-currency.html](E:/SaaS/site/projects/multi-currency.html)). Spoke pages do not link to each other: product, industry, and case-study pages reference modules as plain text, so only the hub pages link to them. The site also disagrees with itself on counts. The homepage grid shows 10 of 12 modules and 9 of 11 industries, and the notes give 54 HTML files against 53 sitemap URLs, so one set of counts should be generated from `site-content.mjs` and reused everywhere.

Machine-readable status matters because assistants summarise the machine layer first. Status appears in the page as Level tags, the pending page, and an "Availability" line, but the llms.txt file and the schema carry none of it. The **Organization** entity has no legal name, no logo, and no `alternateName`, and "ERP Platform" collides with many products, so an assistant cannot tell this company from others. Add a "Status" section to llms.txt, add the FAQ and blog to it, and add a last-updated date. These are changes to the generator and the content file, not to each page by hand. Robots rules are a policy choice: the current `*` allow already lets AI crawlers in, so explicit lines only matter if the business wants to control access.

## Conclusion: sequence the work around trust

The gap is not missing features. It is that the site's trust signals outrun its evidence. A buyer who lands on the homepage gets a clear story and then hits a wall at price, migration, security, and support, and the machine-readable layer tells assistants the product is finished. The sequence that closes the gap fastest is: fix the placeholders and the lead pipeline so a submission is never lost, reconcile every status claim with the pending list, then publish the pricing basis, security and data, migration, support, and first-market pages, which need decisions rather than new code. An honest comparison and a "what is ERP" page can follow once the vendor figures are checked.

Several conclusions rest on inference rather than measurement. No browser run, crawl, Lighthouse check, or AI assistant test was done, and the search-intent ranking reflects reasoning about buyers rather than measured volume. The Google and AI-crawler rules cited in the notes come from memory and need checking against current vendor documentation. The notes also disagree with each other on small points, such as the count of HTML files and the number of homepage modules, so the counts should be re-checked on the built site before the report's numbers are quoted externally.
